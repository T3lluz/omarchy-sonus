# AGENTS.md

How Sonus is put together, for anyone (or any agent) changing it. The user-facing story is in [README.md](README.md).

## Layout

- `Service.qml`: always loaded. Runs `bin/sonus-engine` and talks JSON lines with it; mirrors its state for the panel; owns the `sonus` IPC target; keeps the Ctrl+Alt+V Hyprland bind in `~/.config/hypr/bindings.lua` via `ui/Shortcut.js`.
- `Panel.qml`, `ui/`: the bar entry and panel, once per monitor, all reading the one service. The mixer is a wide panel of rooms (see Panel look) with no empty rows. The top row is a room holding `HeroBlock.qml` (the mark, title, the prompt line, settings) over Game, then `DeviceTile.qml` for the output and the input sharing the rest equally: device names run long, and a channel's column leaves a three-digit figure no room for one. `DeviceList.qml` slides open under them as small rooms on the channels' four columns. Below that, `ChannelTile.qml` for the four channels in one row, each with its fader on top, its equalizer drawer under it, and its bucket of `AppCard.qml`s along the bottom; `NoChannelTile.qml` is a room under them for apps that skip the channels. Every room is `Room.qml` (tint, rise, ripple, click) around `RoomHeader.qml` (pixel icon, name and chevron, a decoding state line, `BigFigure.qml`) and `Fader.qml` (the mute button in the icon column, then `Meter.qml`, the cells that are also the fader). `Drawer.qml` is what a click opens. `Panel.qml` sets the shared sizes (`roomPad`, `roomGap`, `iconColumn`, `textInset`) and `mixerWidth`, wider than the official 380 so four channels fit in a row, and wider than 860 when the longest channel name and a "100" with its mark would not fit side by side (`channelNeed`). Settings stays at 380. `Grip.qml` is the drag sign, `DragHandle.qml` picks an app up. `HoverCard.qml` is what the bar item opens on hover, a `HoverRow.qml` per room. `SonusLogo.qml` draws the mark. `ui/Model.js` is the Rooms logic (tones, block face, pixel type, cells, decode, icon maps, the hover card's rows), `ui/Motion.js` how each icon acts itself out, `ui/Eq.js` the panel's copy of the EQ model, `ui/Audio.js` the panel's words, `ui/Glyphs.js` the Nerd Font glyphs handed to the kit and the shell's volume toast. `ui/` also holds the panel kit (below).
- **Icons** are pixel art from one set, Pixelarticons (MIT), drawn by `PixelIcon.qml` on whole device pixels from `ui/Pixels.js`: never scaled, smoothed or turned off the quarter turns. `scripts/fetch-pixel-icons.py` builds `Pixels.js` (and keeps the source SVGs in `pixels/`), plus the AirPods drawn by hand in the same weight. Add an icon to its `ICONS` list and run it; `--show` prints the grids. `PARTS` splits an icon into the pieces that move on their own (a speaker's waves, a headphone's cups, the slash of `mic-off`), and `LAYERS` adds pixels it shows only while moving (the laptop's typed line, the mic's fill rows); both halve with the icon, and the parts together are exactly the icon, so it rests as drawn. Apps keep their own icons from the icon theme, in their own colours; only a missing one falls back to the pixel `app-windows`.
- **Text** is Monocraft Sonus (OFL 1.1): Monocraft patched by `scripts/patch-font.py` to ten font pixels to the em with an eleven-pixel line, bundled in `assets/fonts` and loaded by one `FontLoader` in `Panel.qml`, which hands its family to everything as `fontFamily`. Every Monocraft text is the kit's `ui/MonoText.qml` (the same file in all four plugins) with a `step` ("small", "body" or "title") and `fontFamily` from the host: it lands each font pixel on a whole number of device pixels for the window's scale and starts the text on a whole device pixel. Never set its font, `renderType`, `antialiasing` or `transform`. Hierarchy is step and colour, never weight (Monocraft's bold is never on the grid): names and rows are body in the text colour, states, hints, captions and headers small and dim (`Qt.tint(fg, Qt.rgba(bg.r, bg.g, bg.b, 0.4))`), page titles title. To size a column before text exists, `Model.monoAdvance(step, dpr)` (a mirror of `MonoText.cellFor`, held to it by a test) gives one character's width. Read the window's `devicePixelRatio`, never `Screen.devicePixelRatio`, which says 2 on a 1.25 monitor. The block face (`BigFigure`) measures its grid on the shell's font instead (`shellFont`), and `Glyph.qml` types its Nerd Font fallback in the shell's font, since Monocraft has no icons; while its kit parent is busy it shows the reload arrows stepping round in quarter turns. Every glyph the panel types must be in Monocraft: one it lacks comes from a fallback font with taller lines, and the row grows and shrinks (the prompt's cursor is `_` and its sign `›` for that reason).
- **The mark** is the app icon's three faders in pixels (`Model.sonusLogo`, drawn by `SonusLogo.qml`): one-cell tracks, knobs three by two, the track lit under each knob and dim over it. In the bar that is the bar's foreground and its dim colour, at the shell's icon size. Muted, the faders are down and all of it dim. In the panel's title room the knobs rise into place on opening and slide down and back on mute (`Model.logoKnobs`), a whole row at a time. A channel's icon must not look like a device's; `tests/model.test.js` checks it, and that every name the QML uses exists.
- **Changing text.** Explanation lives in the hero's prompt line, not in paragraphs: `Panel.heroLine` says what is happening (a drag, an open room, a just-finished move or switch) and otherwise rotates the facts from `Audio.heroPhrases` every 2.8 s, each typed out behind a cursor. A line that follows the pointer (a drag, a fader) shows at once. The prompt keeps room for two lines in the hero's column, so anything with a name in it goes through `Audio.fitted`. State lines under a room's name decode on opening only. Tooltips carry the rest.
- `engine/sonus/`: the engine (Python, stdlib only). `graph.py` renders and runs the filter-chain graph as `sonus-graph.service`; `pipewire.py` reads and moves streams; `eq.py` is the EQ model; `profiles.py` is the per-output-device volume and EQ; `config.py` the settings in `~/.config/sonusdeck/settings.json`, shared with the Sonus desktop app.
- **The spectrum** (`ui/Spectrum.qml`) is the title room's background, through `Room.backdrop`: square pixel cells, lows on the left, each row up fainter (`floorAlpha` 0.1 to `ceilingAlpha`) so the words over it read at any level. Its cells are built once, up to `maxColumns` by `maxRows`, and rows past what the room fits are only hidden, so a room changing height (the device list sliding open) never rebuilds them. `Service.qml` runs cava only while a panel shows the mixer and an app plays (`spectrumWanted`; the panel sets `spectrumBars` to 0 on Settings and the EQ), with its config piped in through `bash -c 'exec cava -p <(printf ...)'` (nothing on disk, and stopping the process stops cava), 15 Hz to 15 kHz at `Model.SPECTRUM.FPS` (30) frames with light smoothing (`noise_reduction = 50`), mono, from the default output's monitor. A new size restarts cava; a cava that exits on its own is started again after 2 s, and its stderr goes to the shell log. cava runs at a fixed sensitivity into a large range (its auto sensitivity overshoots for the first seconds and recovers from a loud passage slowly), and `Model.spectrumFrame` scales each frame against a ceiling that falls back `DECAY` a frame, halving in about 1.4 s, so quiet music still fills the room. Off with reduced motion or the Visualizer switch.
- `engine/sonus/cava.py`: installs cava, and a password prompt never comes out of nowhere. Sonus asks on its own only right after an update: `_report_update` marks the version `pending` in `~/.local/state/sonus/cava.json` when cava is missing and the Visualizer is on, and the "Sonus updated" notification says the prompt is next. The engine after the shell restart asks (`cava_startup`, 4 s after start for the polkit agent), running `pkexec pacman -S --needed --noconfirm cava` in the transient unit `sonus-cava-install` with `--pipe`, so pkexec's stderr comes back. A closed prompt (126/127) stores `declined: true` for good; only Settings' Install button asks again. 127 saying "No authentication agent" showed no prompt, so it tries again a minute later. Only a prompt that really showed records `asked` for that version. Never from an engine that is restarting the shell after an update (`_restarting`): the shell is the polkit agent, and its prompt would die with it. The engine reports cava (`emit_cava`) first thing at startup, on every panel open and on the Visualizer toggle, so a cava installed by hand shows up.
- `engine/sonus/notices.py`: the desktop notifications ("Sonus updated to X", "Sonus couldn't update"), in the Rooms look the guide's Notifications section sets out. They stay real notifications sent with `notify-send`, never a window of our own, which would cover the shell's stack. The picture is a pixel badge drawn in Python for the shell's picture slot in device pixels: `Service.qml` sends `Style.space(40)` (`look`), the engine multiplies it by the focused screen's scale from `hyprctl` and writes the PNG to `~/.local/state/sonus/notices/`. The mark or `warning-diamond` comes from `ui/Pixels.js`; the mark's shape is copied from `Model.sonusLogo`, and a test holds the two together. Updated is the text colour (the title room has no tone) with its meter full; failed is the urgent colour where it reads on the background, with the meter empty. A click opens the panel, or the settings page for a failure. A second failure replaces the first, and a success replaces a failure still up (ids in `~/.local/state/sonus/notices.json`). `omarchy-shell sonus previewNotices` runs that life on made-up values. On a 1.25 screen the slot is a fraction of a device pixel and the shell smooths the picture, so the badge shows a little soft there however it is drawn.
- `bin/sonus-engine`: the entry point. It sets `sys.dont_write_bytecode` so `engine/` never gains a `__pycache__`.

## Test

```bash
node tests/eq.test.js
node tests/audio.test.js
node tests/model.test.js
node tests/shortcut.test.js
node tests/motion.test.js
python3 -B -m unittest discover tests
omarchy-shell sonus status | jq
```

`ui/Eq.js` and `engine/sonus/eq.py` must agree on the bands; `tests/eq.test.js` checks it. `ui/Shortcut.js` owns the Ctrl+Alt+V block; `tests/shortcut.test.js` checks the unbind-before-bind shape.

Sonus keeps its own settings file rather than `shell.json`, so the `WidgetSettings.qml` rule below does not apply to it; the rest does.

## Panel look

The mixer is in the Rooms look Batteries 2.8 set (the brief is `files get key:roomstheme`): each thing a room tinted in its own tone, numbers in a block face, pixel icons, and motion that answers what happens. The settings page keeps the panel kit, so it matches the other plugins. Scaled through `Style`.

- Rooms: padding 10, joined by 3, `Style.cornerRadius`, the tone at 0.065 alpha, 0.11 under the pointer or while open, 0.17 with an outline as a drop target. The keyboard's cursor adds a 1 px outline in the tone, so it reads apart from the pointer.
- Tones (`Model.toneName`, `Model.roomToneHex`): the output and input are the machine (blue), Game is play (green), Chat a connection (cyan), Media media (magenta), Aux yellow; the title room and the apps that skip the channels have none and take the text colour. The theme's colour of that name from its `colors.toml` (read on each open) when it is a colour, else DailyDash's hue, softened 30% toward the text colour. An app card takes its channel's tone. Only the panel and the hover card use tones; the bar keeps the bar's colours.
- A room's header: pixel icon at `font.heading` × 1.25 (20) in the tone, the name in MonoText's body step with a `font.caption` chevron that flips when the room is open, the state in the small step dimmed, the volume as `BigFigure` on the `font.bodySmall` grid on the right. Its mark column (`Model.markFor`) holds `volume-x` or `mic-off` when muted, else `sliders-horizontal` when the channel's EQ shapes the sound; muted also greys the figure. An app's volume is in the pixel type, small, so the channels' figures stay the big ones.
- Meters: ten cells, a tenth each (an app's are thin), track at 0.12, outline at 0.6, no nub. The meter is the fader: press or drag across it in 5% steps, a 1 px tick shows where the pointer would set it, right-click mutes. The input's meter lights the cells it hears (`peak`).
- Clicks open things: a device room its devices, a channel room its equalizer (on or off, and the way into the curve), an app card its channels as buttons in their tones. One open at a time.

Motion, all on one clock (`entry` goes up on each open; `clock` ticks only while a ripple runs) and all off with reduced motion:

- Opening: rooms rise 12 and fade in, 70 ms apart; each meter fills from empty over 680 ms while its figure counts up; state lines decode out of scrambled glyphs; each icon acts itself out; the prompt types itself out.
- Pointer: cells under it give; a click ripples out through the room's meter (Weave's 760 ms), plays the icon again and opens the room, its drawer sliding over 280 ms.
- Changes while open: a level slides to its new value in 420 ms. `Meter.slide()` sets the slide's target when it starts; a target bound to `level` can lag one change behind inside `onLevelChanged`, which drew every wheel step one behind and turned a reversal into one more step the old way. Pages slide 18 px in from their side, and the gear turns in 45° steps.



`SectionHeader`, `StatTile`, `ToggleTile`, `SwitchRow`, `Seg`, `Notice`, `LevelBar`, `OptionRow`, `Caption` and `UpdateSection` in `ui/` are the panel kit: the same files, byte for byte, in ROG Deck, Batteries, Sonus and Server Dashboard, so the four look like one family. Change a kit file in all four or not at all. `Glyph.qml` is the one part each plugin owns: here it looks the glyph up in `Model.PIXEL_GLYPHS` and draws its pixel icon, so the kit's icons match the rooms'. Map every glyph Sonus hands the kit there; `tests/model.test.js` checks `Glyphs.js` against it. The one icon it cannot reach is the "Check now" button inside `UpdateSection`.

Icons (`ui/Motion.js`, played by `PixelIcon.qml`): only the part that should move moves, a whole stroke at a time on the icon's own grid, stepping at most every 40 ms, and every play ends exactly where it began. The laptop types a line on its screen, a speaker's waves fold in and ripple out, headphones and earbuds tap a beat, the mic's capsule lifts, a monitor's scan line sweeps, a TV comes on as a tube does, the controller's pad and buttons press, the chat types its lines, the notes bob, the waveform draws from the left, the slashes and crosses draw across, the EQ knobs slide a step in turn, the download arrow drops into its tray. An icon plays when its room comes in or is clicked, when the pointer reaches a mute button or a channel button, as the device list, the hover card or the settings page shows, and when the state it shows changes: a mute, a level step, a switch of device, an app starting in a channel. A change plays "entering", so what draws in starts from nothing. Live states move only while they last: an app's equaliser bars play while it plays (`playing`, from the stream's state and `pulse.corked`) and stand dimmed when it is paused, on the panel's one `motionTick`, whose `LOOP_TICKS` every bar's period divides; the mic's capsule fills with what it hears. The tick runs only while the mixer is open and something plays. `tests/motion.test.js` checks that every play starts and ends on the icon as drawn, in whole cells, naming only parts that exist.

Panels never cut text: a `Notice` shows all of its message, a `Caption` breaks long words and paths, and a `ToggleTile` label that does not fit shows in full in its tooltip. Every text that can hold a name wraps with `Text.Wrap` (at words, and inside a word only when the word alone is wider than its column, like `alsa_output.usb-…`), never `WordWrap`, which lets such a word run out of its column. Names go through `Model.wrapName` first, which breaks a long raw name (`alsa_output.usb-Focusrite_...`, `steam_app_1245620_EldenRingNightreign`) after `_ . - / :` or at a camelCase hump rather than inside a word; it counts columns, which Monocraft's fixed width makes exact. An app card's name stops at three lines with an ellipsis and shows in full in the card's tooltip. The hover card widens to fit its longest name on one line, up to 380, and ends in a dim line naming the clicks. A room's name keeps its chevron after its last line, and never breaks inside a word: `RoomHeader` keeps room for its longest word and the chevron, and the figure beside it steps down a size if it must; where even that is too little, the chevron steps aside. Check layout changes against long names and three-digit volumes at the panel's real width: `Style.space` scales, so the 860 mixer is about 790 on this machine.

## Rules that are easy to break

- **Settings** are read with `setting(key, fallback)` and saved with `persistSettings({...})`. Both go through `WidgetSettings.qml`, which reads `~/.config/omarchy/shell.json` itself. Never read `settings.foo` directly: the bar's copy turns a list into a Qt sequence that fails `Array.isArray`, and after a plugin reload it can be older than the file. Either one makes a pick look unset, which is how choices "reset after a reboot" or differ between monitors.
- **One widget per monitor.** The bar draws the widget on every screen. Long-running work (a poller, a client, a process) lives in one place, a leader instance or the service, and the others read from it. Never start it per instance.
- **IPC** is answered by whichever monitor's widget registered first, so `open`, `close` and `toggle` go through the shell's `summon`, `hide` and `toggle`, which pick the focused monitor.
- **Nothing may write inside the plugin folder at runtime.** The shell watches it and reloads every plugin when a file appears there. Python sets `sys.dont_write_bytecode`; state goes to `~/.local/state` or `~/.config`.
- **Updates run detached.** `omarchy plugin update` writes into the plugin folder, which reloads the plugin and ends the engine. `updater.start` launches the runner as `sonus-update.service` so it outlives that, and the next engine reports its result from `~/.local/state/sonus/update.json`. That next engine runs the new code and restarts the shell, because the shell's plugin reload keeps QML it has already compiled: without the restart, the old panel stays on screen reading the new version. The runner is the old version's code, so anything an update must do afterwards belongs in the next engine. Never run the update as a child of the engine.
- **Apps are what apps play.** A filter-chain or loopback plays into the hardware through a stream of its own; `pipewire.is_app_stream` keeps those (anything with `node.link-group` and no binary) out of the app list.
- **QML changes need `omarchy restart shell`.** Hot reload can keep the old component cached.
- **Pure logic goes in `ui/Model.js`, `ui/Audio.js` or `ui/Eq.js`** (`.pragma library` files with no QML) so `node tests/*.test.js` can run it. Change it with a test.
- **Pixel art stays sharp.** `Rectangle`s with `antialiasing: false`, positions in whole cells of the icon's own grid (`PixelIcon.cell`), never `scale` or a turn other than a quarter (the gear's eighths are the one exception, and only while it turns).
- Comments say why, in plain sentences. Match the surrounding code.

## Release

Bump `version` in `manifest.json` and the version badge in `README.md` together, run the tests, and describe the change for the person using it in the commit message.
